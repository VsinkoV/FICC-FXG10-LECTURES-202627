## 0. October at a glance

October is the month the **28 Oct FOMC** gets decided. The market already prices about 72% of a hike, so the dollar trades on the *surprise* (Lecture 2). There are three data hurdles before the meeting, and the Iran/oil situation runs as a wild card throughout.

| Date (2026) | Release / event | Why it matters for USD in October |
|---|---|---|
| Wed 30 Sep | Aug PCE (+0.4% m/m expected); Q2 GDP third estimate | The Fed's target gauge sets the tone going into the month |
| Fri 2 Oct | **Sep payrolls**: consensus +100k, unemployment 4.2%, AHE +0.3% m/m | Last payrolls before the FOMC. A beat locks in the hike; a miss (<50k) unwinds it |
| Wed 14 Oct | **Sep CPI** (8:30 ET) | Last CPI before the FOMC; the core reading is key |
| Thu 15 Oct | Sep PPI | Pipeline inflation, feeds into core PCE |
| from ~17 Oct | Fed blackout period begins | No more Fed guidance; the data alone drives pricing |
| Wed 28 Oct | **FOMC decision** (2:00 ET) and Warsh press conference | Hike ≈ priced (small USD gain); hawkish hike (USD up); **hold = the big USD-negative surprise** |
| Fri 30 Oct | BoJ decision (~26% hike priced) | USD/JPY leg of the house view |
| Fri 30 Oct | Month-end rebalancing | Lecture 4 flow: equity gains mean mechanical USD selling |
| Throughout | Treasury buybacks (9 Sep to 4 Nov); Iran/Hormuz headlines; CFTC every Friday | Yield cap (USD−); oil and safe-haven swings (USD±) |

**October scenario grid (for the 1 to 3 month box)**

| Scenario | Trigger | DXY direction | Likely pairs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base: hawkish hike | Payrolls ≥100k, core CPI ≥0.3% m/m, hike on 28 Oct with more signalled | Up, grinding (above 101.3 July high) | EUR/USD towards 1.13 (JPM range); USD/JPY capped near 160 by intervention risk |
| Priced hike, neutral guidance | In-line data | Flat to slightly lower ("buy the rumour, sell the fact") | Range trade |
| Dovish surprise / hold | Soft payrolls, benign CPI, or buybacks escalate | Down sharply | EUR/USD back to 1.16+, USD/JPY lower |
| Risk-off shock | Iran escalation (Brent towards $150) | Up (safe haven), but JPY and CHF outperform | USD/CHF, USD/JPY lower; AUD/USD sharply lower |

## 3. The five structural drivers (Lecture 3), USD side

| Driver | Evidence | Reading for USD |
|---|---|---|
| Policy cycle and real rates | New hiking cycle; dots at 4.1%; real rate positive and widening against the ECB, BoJ and BoC | **Supportive** |
| Terms of trade | US is a net energy exporter; Brent above $100 on the Iran war and Hormuz disruption | Mildly **supportive**, but oil also drives the inflation-expectations story |
| Balance of payments / fiscal | "Global financial arms race" and heavy debt issuance; 10y at 5%; Treasury doubled long-end buybacks (≥$4bn, 9 Sep to 4 Nov); dollar fell ~0.8% on the 19 Aug announcement | **Headwind**: term premium and fiscal dominance risk |
| Valuation | REER 114, near record; ~17% rich to EUR, ~40% rich to JPY on PPP | **Headwind**, but slow-acting |
| Global dollar cycle (smile) | US growth 1.5% but jobs re-accelerating; geopolitical risk-off (Iran) | Both ends of the smile are active: safe haven plus US rate outperformance |

## 7. Draft scaffolding for the page (the four questions)

**1 to 3 months**
1. *What is priced?* About 72% of an October hike; dots at 4.1% by year end.
2. *Candidate view:* USD firm. Payrolls beat, core CPI above consensus, Brent above $100 and the 10y at 5% all point the same way.
3. *Positioning:* DXY spec longs near a 3-month low (+10k) after the record July long was cut. The trade is **not crowded**, so there is room to rebuild longs.
4. *What changes our mind:* a soft 2 Oct payrolls (under 50k) or a hold on 28 Oct; an Iran ceasefire that deflates oil; bigger Treasury buybacks.

**6 to 12 months**
1. *What is priced?* Fed funds plateau near 4.1% through 2027, easing to 3.9% in 2028.
2. *Candidate view:* tension between a positive, rising real rate gap (USD up) and a stretched REER plus fiscal and term-premium risk (USD down).
3. *Where we might disagree:* whether the Fed can outrun a Treasury actively leaning against yields (a Fed–Treasury "at odds" regime).
4. *What changes our mind:* core PCE falling towards 2%, growth below 1%, or the Fed ceding to fiscal pressure.
